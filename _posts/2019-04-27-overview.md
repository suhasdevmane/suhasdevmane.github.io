Traditional conversational agents struggle with heterogeneous smart buildings — devices,
vocabularies, and schemas vary widely, so fixed responses don't generalize. **OntoSage** tackles this
with an agentic, ontology-grounded stack:

- A **LangGraph** hub-and-spoke orchestrator: an LLM classifies intent, a deterministic Python router
  dispatches to the right agent, and an audit trail records every decision.
- **TTL-first** knowledge: a Brick/BACnet ontology in GraphDB plus the **OntoSage Conversational
  Building Vocabulary (OCBV)** — answered via SPARQL first.
- **Live time-series** by UUID (MySQL today, pluggable to Postgres/Timescale/Influx), floor-plan
  geometry (PDF + DWG), and a capability/document knowledge base.
- **Local or API models**, chosen independently for language and embeddings — run fully offline or on
  the API.

Users ask in plain English — *"What's the CO₂ in Room 2.11?"*, *"Any anomalies in HVAC today?"*,
*"Show me floor 3 and how many rooms are there"* — and get grounded, persona-framed, multi-intent
answers, with honest "no data" when a referent isn't modelled.
